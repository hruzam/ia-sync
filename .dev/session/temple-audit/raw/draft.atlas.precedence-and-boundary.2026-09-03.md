# FINDING 1 — the precedence stone

## (a) LOCATION — argued, not picked: `temple/precedence.md`, NOT `raw.canon/`, NOT `doctrine.md`

Three candidates, two rejected on structural grounds:

- **`raw.canon/` — REJECT (circularity).** raw.canon is a *domain*: a bucket of promoted invariants
  (`canon.mail-protocol`, `canon.context-economy`, …), each terminal in its own scope, each carrying
  `expresses_as`. The precedence stone must *order* every domain — including raw.canon itself (a
  `canon.*` entry can collide with Sella or a flag). A law that orders raw.canon cannot be *filed inside*
  raw.canon without becoming peer to the things it ranks → no apex, circular. Oraculum's "canon from
  ashes" instinct is right about *tone* (short, receipts-backed, grows on need) but wrong about *tier*:
  the apex cannot sit inside one of its own domains.
- **`temple/doctrine.md` — REJECT (wrong genus).** doctrine.md declares itself in line 9: *"Read it as
  advice, not law."* It is the architect's mentor-text — portable skeleton, half-life caveats, adapt-
  don't-obey. The precedence stone is the exact opposite genus: the *one hard law* the temple holds, the
  thing that is NOT advice. Folding a binding precedence order into a document whose whole frame is
  "advice, re-verify, adapt" dilutes the one clause that must never be adapted.
- **`temple/precedence.md` — ADOPT.** The temple layer is the sovereign root (AGENTS.md: "READ FIRST …
  the cross-project sovereign source"). It already holds three governance genera side by side: `doctrine.md`
  (advice), `decisions/` (gaveled locks), `roster/system-map/file-tree` (weather). The precedence stone is
  a *fourth, distinct* genus: the **order among all law-bearing documents**. Domain-neutral by
  construction, it belongs at the domain-neutral root, beside doctrine — not inside a domain it ranks.
  Adoption recorded as the next decision (0013), so the stone has a durable named address AND a gaveled
  lock; every WINS clause cites the *named file*, never the decision number.

# FINDING 2 — Sella ↔ session-shape boundary

Your proposed split is correct and I accept it. The boundary paragraph BOTH documents carry:

```markdown
## Boundary — Sella and the session-shape canon

Two disciplines, one seam, no overlap:

- **Sella** governs PRIMITIVE-BUILDING — how an agent, skill, hook, or relay is authored: the
  stochastic-compiler laws, description budgets, the smallest-native-primitive rule, verify-before-
  promote. It answers *"is this primitive well-made?"*
- **The session-shape canon** (`raw.guides/{runbook,status,PAD,bus,gloss}`) governs SESSION SHAPE —
  how work is framed, positioned, exchanged, and closed: one gate, one present position, the bus
  cycle, the promotion boundary. It answers *"is this session well-run?"*

A primitive is BUILT under Sella and RUN inside a session shaped by this canon. Where they meet — a
skill that drives a RUNBOOK, say — Sella owns the skill's construction, this canon owns the session
the skill produces. Neither wins over the other; they rank different objects. Cross-domain collisions
that survive this seam go to `temple/precedence.md`.
```

This retires Sella's current "wins over sibling builder guides" as a *cross-domain* claim: against a
session-shape guide it does not win, it *abstains* (different object). It keeps winning against other
*builder* guides (codex-builder-user) — same domain, where it is genuinely terminal. So Sella:9's clause
narrows from "sibling builder guides" to "sibling *builder* guides" explicitly, and gains the subordination
line from finding 1(c).
