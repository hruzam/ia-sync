# COUNTER-SIGN — cSharp fan-out turns

## COUNTER-SIGN

**YES-WITH-AMENDMENTS.** Four amendments, one of them blocking (REVISE). The draft carried
the assessment's findings faithfully and improved two of them (§10 native boundary, §11 test).
I am signing the shape; the amendments are inside it, not around it.

## Blocking semantic problems

**1 — REVISE is not terminal.** §4's example `join_when` lists `ACCEPT, REVISE, STOP, or
BLOCKED` as terminal. REVISE means *the work is not done; a corrective cycle follows*. A head
that joins on a REVISE joins a turn whose work is open, and the corrective cycle then lands in
the *next* turn by §8 — which splits one coordination decision across two turns for no reason.
Amend: terminal = `ACCEPT | STOP | BLOCKED`. A REVISE opens a corrective cycle that **inherits
the turn** and is appended to `join_when:`. This is the one place the draft would produce a
wrong join on a normal day, not on a failure day.

**2 — At most one open turn per session.** §7 says "the next turn" and implies serial turns
but never states it. State it: **one open turn, one `join_when:`, one `next:`.** This is the
strongest anti-scheduler law available and it costs one sentence. Two open turns = two join
conditions = a plan, which STATUS forbids. If the head wants a second fan-out before the first
joins, it joins partially first (BLOCKED what it will not wait for) and opens the next.

**3 — `deferred` must never point at a closed turn.** A late VERDICT for cycle 08 arriving
after turn 03 has joined cannot say `deferred: turn 03` — that names a join that will never
happen; the edge dangles forever. Amend: a late VERDICT writes `no + reason: turn 03 joined;
finding carried as curvature/hold into turn 04` or `deferred: turn 04` if 04 is open. Rule of
thumb: `deferred` may only name the currently open turn.

**4 — `awaiting:` must name the seat when it is not `to:`.** With a crossed witness,
`awaiting: verdict` waits on `assay`, not on `coder-a`. A 03:00 cSharp needs to know whom to
poke. Amend the vocabulary: `awaiting: return` (implies `to:` seat) · `awaiting: verdict <seat>`.

## STATUS pressure — the constraint Asymmetry asked me to attack

The brake *"retype a delegated entry only when the difference matters to a fresh cSharp"* has
the right intent and is the wrong kind of rule. It is **judgement-bound, not observable** — the
same defect the STATUS GUIDE already rejected in the "never append" prohibition: *append
behavior is not observable from the prose*. "Matters to recovery" is not observable either; two
honest heads will disagree, and neither can be checked. A rule that cannot be checked is show.

Replace it with a **vocabulary bound**, which is structural and checkable:

- A `delegated:` line has exactly these keys: `cycle · to · turn · awaiting · carrier` (carrier
  only for a human relay; native/tunnel/mail derive from the RUNBOOK tuple).
- `awaiting:` has exactly two values: `return` | `verdict <seat>`.
- **No timestamps, no "sent", no "acknowledged", no progress, no notes.** If a fact does not fit
  the vocabulary it does not go in `delegated:`; it goes in `holds:` if it constrains, or nowhere.

Then the question *when to rewrite* needs no new answer — existing law already says *after every
verified transition and before yielding the seat*. A dashboard cannot grow inside a field that
cannot hold dashboard words. Keep Asymmetry's sentence as the *why* under the vocabulary, not as
the rule.

Verdict on the question asked: with the vocabulary bound and the one-open-turn law,
`delegated:` is recovery state. Without them, the draft's own brake would not prevent a
scheduler; it would only make the scheduler's author feel guilty.

## Deferred edge

Sufficient, with one recovery law added. The new failure surface is the gap **between a VERDICT
landing on disk and the head retyping `delegated:`**. Power fails there: STATUS says
`07 awaiting: verdict assay`, disk has `07.assay.verdict.md · deferred: turn 03`. A fresh cSharp
must treat this as ordinary curvature — **disk beats STATUS, difference recorded** — not as a
contradiction that stalls it.

So during a fan-out, `recovery_probe:` is fixed by law, not chosen: *reconcile every
`delegated:` line against `_bus/NN.*.verdict.md` presence and `disposition:`; every mismatch is
the partial state.* That is exactly the STATUS GUIDE's "test the mechanism, not the residue" —
here the mechanism is the verdict file's contents, not its presence. One sentence in the STATUS
amendment; it makes the deferred edge cold-resumable by construction.

Gate-close case: a deferred VERDICT never joined (gate closed on a partial turn) is listed in
the closing STATUS as curvature and in the promotion manifest. Never silently pruned.

## Turn boundary

"New turn after join" is mechanically strong enough **once amendments 1–3 hold**: REVISE stays
in-turn, only one turn is open, and `deferred` cannot name a closed turn. With those, every
late or corrective artifact has exactly one legal `turn:` and the head never has to decide by
taste. Without amendment 3, late returns are the hole.

One clarification worth writing down: `turn:` orders coordination decisions; `NN` orders
exchanges. They are allowed to interleave (cycle 09 corrective in turn 03 after cycle 08's turn-04
POINT exists). Say so, or someone will "fix" the interleaving and renumber.

## Experimental test — one additional failure injection

**Kill the head between a VERDICT landing and the STATUS retype, then cold-resume a fresh
cSharp.** This is the only seam that is genuinely new — `deferred` + `delegated:` reconciliation
under the 03:00 standard. Relay mismatch (§8) is detectable by design and can wait for a second
run. If a second injection is cheap: inject one REVISE, to prove amendment 1 in the field.

## Canon destination

Confirm the four files with **one relocation**: the verifier/status_owner boundary (§6) is an
*authority* law, and the STATUS GUIDE says authority lives in `runbook/GUIDE.md` and is not
reproduced elsewhere. Put it beside `status_owner` in the RUNBOOK GUIDE (or `res/token-economy.md`,
which already defines `status_owner`); `bus/GUIDE.md` carries only what `deferred` means and
points. Otherwise the fix for duplicated authority is itself duplicated — the audit's own scar.

## FYI — the relay, from majkee's note (in scope: it is workflow shape)

Yesterday's run was the join done by hand: ask cSharp for prompts, paste them, collect replies
into one batch per turn. The candidate canon moves exactly that batch into `join_when:` and the
head's rewrite. For the relay to shrink to *one path per paste*, one line in RUNBOOK
`prompt-N` does it: **"your work arrives as an absolute path to a POINT; read it, write your
RETURN to its `return_to:`."** Then the carrier pastes a path, nothing else; the POINT is already
the whole brief. That is the least bureaucracy the file plane allows, and it needs no new law —
only the prompts pointing at the files, which the RUNBOOK GUIDE already requires.
