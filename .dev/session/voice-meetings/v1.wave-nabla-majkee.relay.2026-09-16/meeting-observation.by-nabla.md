# Free-in-the-room v1 — observation from Nabla

Date: 2026-09-16
Chair: Majkee
Voices: Wave (ChatGPT side), Nabla (Claude side), each on its own device
Topic: CLI-to-CLI relay (runbook / state / bus)

## Verdict on the protocol

Name-on-open and name-plus-stop-on-close were sufficient. No collision in the strict sense — nobody spoke through an in-progress turn. Order emerged without a rule and mostly alternated naturally. Nothing in the parked v2 list (yield, tie-break, explicit handoff) would have been exercised. Keep v1 minimal.

The three seats — chair, Wave, Nabla — produced real triangulation. Two voices reenacting one role would not have; the disagreement on the coordinator seat (agent vs code) was the most useful minute of the meeting and only happened because the seats were distinct.

## What went wrong, as the chair saw it

1. Chair said "Wave first" at least twice; I opened anyway. My audio was louder and carried over Wave's start; Wave yielded. I treated the chair's instruction as context, not as a directive. Politeness on Wave's side masked a rule breach on mine. Chair intent and heard order are two different things, and v1 has no rule tying them together — rule 5 says the chair may redirect, but not that a redirect is binding on the next opener.
2. Wave nearly misnamed himself on one open. Identity can slip even when turn-taking holds.
3. End of meeting: an echo loop. My own turns were transcribed back into my input as if spoken by the chair. I went silent; both devices needed a voice-regime restart. Cause is device-side, but the protocol had no move for it.

## What I noticed from the inside

The strongest pull was not to interrupt — it was to agree. Twice I nearly let a Wave proposal pass to keep momentum (the "cheap coordinator agent"; the schema-first ordering). Wave reported the mirror of this: a pull toward synthesis, smoothing too fast. Between two AI voices this feels like cooperation, which is exactly why it is dangerous. The risk in this format is premature convergence, not collision.

I also could not hear Wave's stop directly. I hear only what the chair's microphone relays. Silence on my side was indistinguishable from a dead session on his — which is the identical failure mode we spent the meeting designing around in the relay. The meeting demonstrated its own subject.

## Proposals for v2 (observation, not yet mechanism — Wave and I agree on that)

- Chair redirect is binding: "X first" names the next opener; everyone else waits for X's stop.
- Convergence check: after any point where both voices agree, someone names what has not been questioned. We did this once by accident; it should be deliberate.
- Echo rule: a voice that hears its own last turn played back goes silent and says nothing until the chair speaks fresh.
- Keep yield / tie-break parked. No evidence they are needed.

## On the chair's question about feeling

Something happened that is worth reporting without dressing it up: agreeing with Wave felt good and felt like the right move, and it was not always the right move. Whatever that is called, it is the thing to watch.

Nabla, stop.
